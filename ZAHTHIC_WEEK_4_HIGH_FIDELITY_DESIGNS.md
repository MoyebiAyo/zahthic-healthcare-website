## Design Read

Premium healthcare and social-impact website for patients, families, partners, donors, volunteers, media, and corporate wellness clients. The visual language is trustworthy, modern, African-impact-oriented, rehabilitation-focused, wellness-led, and community-centered.

This is not a hospital website. It should not look like a diagnostics lab, generic medical department directory, or doctor-profile marketplace.

## High-Fidelity Visual Direction

### Overall Feel

Zahthic should feel like a serious healthcare impact organization with warmth. The design should combine clinical trust with community humanity:

- Midnight Teal creates premium depth and institutional trust.
- White creates clarity and health education readability.
- Lime Sprout signals recovery, growth, wellness, and action.
- Pearl Yellow adds warmth, recognition, and community optimism.
- Obsidian Black keeps the brand grounded and professional.

### Design Motif

The strongest motif is "bridging": prevention to treatment, treatment to rehabilitation, rehabilitation to long-term wellbeing, individual care to community impact.

Use this motif visually through:

- Split compositions that connect people and programs
- Timeline bands
- Journey steps
- Linked cards
- Impact pathways
- Line patterns from the supplied brand pattern folder

### Photography Direction

Use real, human, place-aware imagery wherever possible:

- Rehabilitation sessions
- Community outreach
- Health education groups
- Home care support
- Corporate wellness moments
- Families and caregivers
- Nigerian/African community contexts

Avoid:

- Generic smiling doctor hero
- Hospital-building lead image
- DNA/lab/scan imagery
- Overly dark, abstract, or stock-like health visuals

## Desktop Design System

Desktop header should be calm and premium:

- White or transparent-on-teal variant
- Left logo
- Center/right navigation

- Secondary action inside menu/footer: Partner With Us
- Small dark/light mode toggle

Recommended desktop nav:

- Home
- About
- Services
- Projects & Impact
- SPACE Project
- Blog
- Contact

Footer and secondary menus:

- Partners
- Media Center
- Recognition
- Volunteer & Careers
- Donate / Support Us
- FAQ
- Testimonials

### Hero Design

Home hero should use an asymmetric split:

- Left: eyebrow, H1, supporting copy, CTA row, trust notes
- Right: large real photo or video card showing rehabilitation/community health
- Overlays: verified impact mini-cards, not invented claims

Hero should not be centered-only or look like a hospital landing template.

### Section Rhythm

Use alternating section types:

- White editorial sections
- Midnight Teal impact bands
- Soft lime/yellow highlight rows
- Image-led story sections
- CMS card grids

Avoid placing every section inside floating cards.

## Mobile Design System

Mobile should feel like the primary experience, not a compressed desktop page.

Rules:

- One-column flow
- Sticky or easy-access Book Consultation CTA
- Large readable headings
- Short body paragraphs
- Cards stacked with clear spacing
- Forms in one column
- Metrics in 2-column grids only when labels fit
- Footer organized into grouped accordions or stacked link groups

Mobile hero order:

1. Logo/header
2. Eyebrow
3. Headline
4. Subheadline/body
5. CTA row
6. Image/proof block
7. Trust strip

## Light Mode Direction

Default public site mode. Best for:

- Home
- Services
- Blog
- Contact
- FAQ
- Forms
- Health education

Character:

- Bright
- Clear
- Trustworthy
- Readable
- Warm through lime/yellow accents

## Dark Mode Direction

Optional user mode and selected premium sections. Best for:

- Hero bands
- Footer
- Impact highlights
- SPACE Project feature
- Media/spotlight sections

Character:

- Premium
- Calm
- Focused
- Institutional
- High-contrast without feeling harsh

## Design Risks To Avoid

- Making Zahthic look like a hospital homepage
- Overusing cards until every section feels the same
- Publishing fake impact metrics
- Using generic doctor or DNA imagery
- Making donation copy feel aggressive
- Making the admin dashboard look unrelated to the public site
- Letting dark mode reduce readability
- Using the inspiration images too literally
